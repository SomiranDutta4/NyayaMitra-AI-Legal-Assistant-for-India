### Understanding your situation
Your landlord locked you out of your room on 1 November 2026, kept your laptop and ₹30,000 deposit without giving a reason, and threatened to beat you if you go to the police. Since you did not vacate voluntarily and are currently staying safely at a friend's place, you have legal remedies under both civil and criminal laws.

### Where you stand legally
- Your landlord dispossessed you of your immovable property without your consent and otherwise than in due course of law when he locked your room. [1]
- The landlord is required to let you hold the property peacefully during the lease if you perform your obligations, and he cannot lock you out or keep your belongings. [2]
- Threatening to beat you if you go to the police constitutes criminal intimidation, which is punishable under criminal law. [3]
- Withholding your laptop and refusing to return your deposit or belongings without consent can amount to criminal breach of trust or theft. [4][5]

### Your options
1. **File a Police Complaint (FIR)** — You can report the criminal intimidation, withholding of your laptop, and illegal lockout at a police station under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023. If the police refuse to record the information, you may send it to the Superintendent of Police. [6][3][4]
2. **File a Civil Suit to Recover Possession** — You can file a suit to recover possession of your room without having to prove title, as you were dispossessed without your consent otherwise than in due course of law. [1]
3. **File a Civil Money Suit for Deposit Recovery** — You can file a civil suit to recover your ₹30,000 security deposit, which has a general limitation period of three years from when the right to sue accrues. [7]

### Time limits
- A suit to recover possession after illegal dispossession must be filed within six months from the date of dispossession (1 November 2026). [1]
- A civil money suit for recovering your security deposit can be filed within three years from when the right to sue accrues. [7]

### What to do next
1. Gather all evidence related to your tenancy, rent receipts, and the locked room.
2. Approach the local police station in Bengaluru to file a complaint regarding the threats and your kept laptop.
3. If the police refuse to record the information, send the complaint details to the Superintendent of Police.
4. Consult a local advocate in Bengaluru to evaluate filing a suit for recovery of possession and your security deposit.
5. Maintain a record of all communications with your landlord.
