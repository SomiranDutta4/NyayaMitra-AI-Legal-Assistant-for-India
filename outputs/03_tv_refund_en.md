### Understanding your situation
You ordered a Sony LED TV from Amazon for ₹45,000 on 2 October 2026, which arrived with a broken screen. The seller refused replacement and closed your ticket, amounting to a defect in goods and an unfair trade practice.

### Where you stand legally
- The broken screen on your television qualifies as a defect in goods, which is any fault, imperfection, or shortcoming in the quality or standard of goods required under contract. [1]
- The seller's refusal to take back the defective goods and refund or replace them constitutes an unfair trade practice under the law. [2]
- Since the amount paid for the goods is ₹45,000 (which does not exceed fifty lakh rupees), your complaint falls within the jurisdiction of the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission. [3]

### Your options
1. **District Consumer Commission Complaint** — You can file a formal consumer complaint before the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (which can be done electronically through the e-Daakhil portal) to seek replacement of the goods, return of the price paid with interest, or compensation. [4][5][3]

### Time limits
- You must file your consumer complaint within two years from 2 October 2026, the date on which the cause of action arose. [6]

### What to do next
1. Check Amazon's platform details for the designated grievance officer to ensure all digital communications and ticket closures are documented.
2. Organize your purchase invoice, payment proof of ₹45,000 dated 2 October 2026, and photographs of the broken screen.
3. Draft your consumer complaint summary detailing the purchase and the seller's refusal to replace the defective TV.
4. Access the e-Daakhil portal to prepare for filing your complaint before the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission.
